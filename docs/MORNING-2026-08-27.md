```sql
begin;

-- The two keys a BHBC coach must never write. The PT keeps medical; owner and
-- staff keep everything, since is_trainer()/is_staff() short-circuit the test.
create or replace function public.bhbc_coach_write_blocked(k text)
returns boolean
language sql
stable
security definer
set search_path = public
as $$
  select
    public.is_bhbc_coach()
    and not coalesce(public.is_bhbc_pt(), false)
    and not coalesce(public.is_trainer(), false)
    and not coalesce(public.is_staff(), false)
    and k in ('expo-bhbc-medical', 'expo-exercises')
$$;

drop policy if exists store_bhbc_coach_write_ceiling_update on public.store;
create policy store_bhbc_coach_write_ceiling_update on public.store
  as restrictive
  for update
  to authenticated
  using (not public.bhbc_coach_write_blocked(key))
  with check (not public.bhbc_coach_write_blocked(key));

drop policy if exists store_bhbc_coach_write_ceiling_insert on public.store;
create policy store_bhbc_coach_write_ceiling_insert on public.store
  as restrictive
  for insert
  to authenticated
  with check (not public.bhbc_coach_write_blocked(key));

drop policy if exists store_bhbc_coach_write_ceiling_delete on public.store;
create policy store_bhbc_coach_write_ceiling_delete on public.store
  as restrictive
  for delete
  to authenticated
  using (not public.bhbc_coach_write_blocked(key));

commit;
```

## The shot analyzer — measured, and the honest answer

`docs/shot-analyzer-next-2026-08-27.md`.

Three runs of your 11-shot clip returned **11, then 10, then 9**. The detector is
deterministic; the capture is not — the browser presents ~16 fps of a 60 fps
clip while MediaPipe runs, and a release landing in a gap is never seen.

**That is the biggest problem with the tool**, ahead of scoring or the ball
track, because the count is the first number you look at.

I did NOT tune any detection threshold against it — two of the three runs find
all 11 with the same thresholds, so loosening a gate would trade a missed shot
for a phantom one. Instead there is now an opt-in deterministic capture mode to
measure the fix before committing to it. It is slower; whether that trade is
worth it is your call.

**And then I measured the fix.** Deterministic capture — stepping the clip with
seeks instead of reading a playing video — found **all 11 shots first try**, on
2,632 frames instead of 741 (58 fps effective vs 16). Ball data roughly doubled
too: 25–42 samples a shot against 13–20.

Then I ran it a second time to check it wasn't luck. **Every one of the 11 shots
came back at the identical millisecond, with identical ball-sample counts** — and
that second run was competing with another audit hammering the same browser
(three of *that* audit's routes timed out from the load). The capture didn't
care. That is the whole difference from the default path.

The price: **607 seconds of capture against 161** — about 3.8× slower, roughly
ten minutes for your 45-second clip.

**That is the decision:** a ~10-minute analysis whose shot count you can trust,
or a ~3-minute one that answers 9, 10 or 11. For a tool whose first number IS
the count, run once a session, I think the slower one is right — but it is your
call, and it is one flag: `captureShotFrames(src, { deterministic: true })`.

There is also an untested middle path: run only the coarse pass (the one that
finds the shots, 255 s) deterministically and leave the fine pass alone. ~6
minutes, should fix the count, ball data stays sparse.

**And I found why shot 4's ball never tracks.** It stayed rejected even with
3.5× the frames, so I dumped the actual video frames and looked. **There are two
balls in the air.** At the moment it calls shot 4's release, your previous shot
is still airborne near its apex — and you are holding the next ball at your
chest. The tracker locks onto the previous ball, which by definition barely
rises because it is already at the top of its arc. Hence "it barely rose" on a
rep where a ball is plainly visible in every frame.

That is why the other ten are fine: in those, the previous ball has landed
before the next release. Shot 4 is the one rep where two overlap. The fix
belongs in the origin constraint that is supposed to reject an arc not starting
at your hand — I swept it and confirmed it is the lever, but did not change it,
because tightening alone does not produce a good arc either and guessing the
value would trade one broken shot for a broken threshold on the other ten.

**And one reassurance, because that finding implied something worse.** If the
release moment were detected early in general, every release angle and every
timing number in the tool would be measured at the wrong instant. I pulled the
real frames at three detected releases and looked: shots 1 and 5 both show the
ball at your hand with the arm extending — exactly right. Shot 4 is the
exception, not the rule. The scorecard's angles are measured where they should
be.

**One caveat, and it matters before you act on this.** Those runs were on a
machine *I* was loading — by 03:40 the debug browser had 29 tabs open from the
sweeps I had been running all night. The variance is real and the mechanism is
measured, but **the spread is an upper bound, not what you would see analysing
one clip on a quiet phone.** You may well get 11 every time. Worth three runs on
an idle machine before deciding the deterministic mode is worth its cost.
