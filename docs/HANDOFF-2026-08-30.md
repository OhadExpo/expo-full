## The shot analyzer: what was actually wrong, and what is proven now

Ohad's complaints were "17 shots, the analyzer found 15", "the count changes",
and "2 minutes per clip". None of it was a detection threshold. Four separate
defects, each found by measurement:

### 1. The count moved between runs — frame delivery, not detection

Two causes, both fixed:

- **Nothing waited for the video to buffer.** `preload='auto'` was set and the
  coarse pass — the pass that decides the count — started as soon as metadata
  arrived, so playback raced the download. Cold cache gave 7 shots, warm gave
  17. `awaitBuffered()` finishes the download first. (`5250c83`)
- **Frames dropped under CPU load were never recovered.**
  `requestVideoFrameCallback` only fires for frames the browser actually
  presents; under load it presents fewer, `skipRatio` stays 0 because nothing
  was *re*-presented, and any rep inside the gap is never bracketed. The gaps
  are now found in the timestamps that did arrive and re-read by exact seek — a
  clean pass finds none and pays nothing. (`0539c3b`)

### 2. Zero shots on real footage — it tracked the wrong person

`pickSubject` scored `h*2 - near*3 + vis*0.5`, where `h` is bounding-box
height, so it preferred whoever was **largest in frame** — on real footage,
whoever stands nearest the camera. Ohad's clips contain exactly one person, so
this was never exercised. On a downloaded clip that plainly shows a player
shooting, the tracker locked onto a foreground bystander and returned **0**.
Bodies clipped by the frame edge now take a penalty (a penalty, not a reject —
a shooter who steps briefly over the edge should lose to a fully visible
alternative, not vanish mid-rep).

**Measured: that clip went from 0 shots to 6.** (`0539c3b`)

### 3. A phantom shot in low light — a definition bug, not a threshold

Found by the variant suite: a darkened copy returned **18** where every other
encode returns 17. The runs agree on all seventeen real releases to within
~400 ms; the extra sat at 79,250 ms in a clip ending at ~79,500 ms — the camera
being switched off, read as a shot. The follow-through checkpoint wants the arm
held 300 ms after release, so a release with 250 ms of clip left can never
satisfy its own scorecard, yet it still counted. Now rejected. (`92bc6e4`)

**Measured after the fix: v_dark 18 → 17, v_ref unchanged at 17.**

### 4. Ball readings — STILL NOT WORKING, and this is the honest state

**0 of 17 reps produce a launch angle.** Two things were fixed and neither was
enough:

- `ascentMissing` fired whenever the hand sat below the first tracked blob,
  which is true on essentially every shot — release is labelled the instant the
  ball leaves and the tracker needs frames to lock on. Every rep returned a null
  angle and the screen said "the ball could not be followed" even when the arc
  fitted at r² 0.999 over 10 samples. Now only refuses past 4 ball widths.
  (`fdfa116`)
- `maxMiss` was 1, so a track died after a SINGLE undetected frame — at release,
  when the ball is fastest and most motion-blurred, which is precisely when
  detection is least reliable. The stats showed it plainly: 21 seeds, 21
  `tooShort`, nothing kept. At `maxMiss = 2`, tracks survive: kept 3, 3, 2, 10,
  10, 8, 24. Shot count verified unchanged at 17.

**Where it now fails:** tracks persist and then fail the physics gate — seven
reps report `not falling like a projectile` (`quad.a < -50`, i.e. no real
downward curvature). So the tracker is following something that is not a ball in
flight, or the arc visible inside the 700 ms post-release window is too short to
show gravity. That is computer-vision work, not a threshold to nudge, and
nudging the gate to force readings through would manufacture numbers rather than
measure them.

Meanwhile the UI stops being vague about it: a rep whose ball WAS followed but
had already left the hand says exactly that, instead of claiming it could not be
followed. (`5e057fa`)

`ascentMissing` fired whenever the hand sat below the first tracked blob —

## The test set, and why random clips cannot test a detector

You do not know how many shots are in a clip off the internet, so a wrong
answer is indistinguishable from a right one. Of ten pulled by search, **three
were usable footage at all** — the rest were a coaching talking-head, a casual
outdoor scene, and a chest-up framing with no knees in shot.

So the suite derives variants from the one clip whose answer is known (17) and
varies what a phone actually varies. **9 of 9 return exactly 17:**

| variant | shots | what it tests |
|---|---|---|
| v_ref | 17 | reference encode |
| v_30fps | 17 | half the frame rate |
| v_24fps | 17 | 24 fps |
| v_480 | 17 | an older phone |
| v_dark | 17 | a gym at night *(was 18 before `92bc6e4`)* |
| v_bright | 17 | a court at noon |
| v_mirror | 17 | filmed from the other side |
| v_wide | 17 | stood further back |
| v_lowq | 17 | heavily compressed |

Outside footage, reported but never scored because the truth is unknown:
`clip02` 6 shots (was 0), `clip08` 1.

Rebuild with `node scripts/make-shot-variants.mjs`, grade with
`node scripts/score-variants.mjs <log>`.

  setting and 58 on another is a different coaching verdict for the same

  to buy time. One earlier attempt at halving the *coarse* pass broke detection

## Ball tracking — root-caused 2026-08-30 (morning), with numbers

The detector was never the problem. Replaying the candidate blobs from a real
run offline (`scripts/probe-ball-size.mjs`, no browser needed) shows the ball is
detected cleanly on every frame of the flight:

```
t=0    312,66      t=217  364,23  <- apex      t=534  424,93
t=100  337,35      t=350  391,34               t=767  463,233
t=184  352,25      t=450  409,60               t=850  481,272
```

Nineteen blobs, one per frame, consistent width 42-54 per-mille, x rising
monotonically 312 -> 481, y falling to an apex then accelerating downward. The
fitted downward acceleration is **1112 per-mille/s^2 against an expected 1104 —
a ratio of 1.01**. That is real gravity to within one percent, which is proof
these blobs are the basketball and that the arc is fully measurable.

### Defect 1 — a threshold of mine, missed by 0.2

`ascentMissing` refused the arc because the ball sits **4.2 ball widths** above
the hand when tracking begins and last night's cutoff was 4. The camera is
framed on the shooter, so the ball's ascent happens mostly ABOVE the frame on
every rep — that is normal footage, not bad footage.

Fixed by refusing on evidence rather than on a gap: once the apex is inside the
tracked window the parabola is fully determined by a curve we watched turn over,
so evaluating it back at the release instant is exactly what the fit is for. The
gap rule still applies when no apex was seen and only a fragment is in hand.

### Defect 2 — a unit bug in every vertical gate (NOT yet fixed)

`expected = 9.81 * (ballPx / BALL_DIAMETER_M)` takes the ball's **width**,
normalised by frame WIDTH, and compares it against `quad.a`, derived from y,
normalised by frame HEIGHT. On a 720x1280 portrait clip those units differ by
0.5625, so every vertical gate is wrong by the frame's aspect ratio:

| | ratio to real gravity |
|---|---|
| as the engine computes it | **0.63** |
| aspect-corrected by hand | **1.01** |

It survives here only because the gate is loose (0.45 to 2.2). On a landscape
clip the error runs the other way. The rise gate (`climb < 1.2 * ballPx`) and
the ascent gap share the same mistake — all three compare a vertical distance
against a width-derived ball size.

Fixing it means threading the frame aspect into `launchAngle` and converting the
ball's width into vertical units before any of those comparisons. Deliberately
left for a separate change: it moves three gates at once and the 48 assertions
in `verify-ball-track.mjs` are calibrated against the current behaviour, so it
needs its own measurement rather than being smuggled in beside the apex fix.
